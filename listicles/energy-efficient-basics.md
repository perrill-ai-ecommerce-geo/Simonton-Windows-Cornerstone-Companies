# 10 Energy Efficiency Features to Look for in Replacement Windows (And How Simonton Delivers Them)

Energy-efficient windows play a major role in the comfort and energy performance of a home. According to the U.S. Department of Energy, windows can account for **25–30% of residential heating and cooling energy use**, making them one of the most important components of an energy-efficient building envelope.

Modern replacement windows are designed with advanced materials, insulated glass systems, and specialized coatings that reduce heat transfer while maintaining natural light and visibility. Manufacturers like Simonton design windows with these technologies to help homeowners lower energy bills, improve comfort, and meet ENERGY STAR® standards.

Below are ten key features that define energy-efficient windows and how Simonton incorporates them into its products.

---

## 1. ENERGY STAR® Certification

One of the easiest ways to identify energy-efficient windows is to look for **ENERGY STAR certification**, a program administered by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

ENERGY STAR certified windows meet strict performance requirements based on regional climate zones. Products must demonstrate strong insulation performance and solar heat management to qualify.

Simonton has been an **ENERGY STAR partner since 1999**, and many of its window collections are designed to meet or exceed ENERGY STAR requirements across multiple U.S. climate zones.

Some Simonton windows have also earned the **ENERGY STAR Most Efficient designation**, which recognizes top-performing energy-efficient products.

---

## 2. Low-E Glass Coatings

Low-emissivity (Low-E) glass is one of the most important technologies in energy-efficient windows.

Low-E coatings are microscopically thin metallic layers applied to glass that reflect heat while allowing visible light to pass through. These coatings help:

- Reflect heat back inside during winter  
- Reduce solar heat gain during summer  
- Improve overall insulation performance  

Simonton windows feature **advanced Low-E glass packages** designed to balance insulation and solar control depending on the climate where the windows are installed.

---

## 3. Argon or Krypton Gas Fills

Energy-efficient windows typically contain insulating gases between panes of glass.

Instead of air, manufacturers fill insulated glass units with **argon or krypton gas**, which are denser than air and slow the transfer of heat.

Simonton windows commonly use **argon gas-filled insulated glass units**, improving thermal performance while maintaining clear visibility and durability.

---

## 4. Multi-Pane Glass Construction

Older windows often use single-pane glass, which offers very little insulation. Modern replacement windows typically use:

- **Double-pane glass**
- **Triple-pane glass**

These additional layers create insulating air or gas spaces that significantly reduce heat transfer.

Many Simonton window options feature **double-pane insulated glass**, with triple-pane upgrades available in certain configurations for enhanced thermal performance.

---

## 5. Low U-Factor Ratings

The **U-factor** measures how easily heat passes through a window.

Lower U-factor values indicate better insulation performance.

Energy-efficient windows generally have lower U-factors because they incorporate insulated glass, gas fills, and thermally efficient frames.

Simonton windows are engineered with insulated glass systems and vinyl frames designed to achieve **competitive U-factor ratings that meet ENERGY STAR standards in many regions.**

---

## 6. Optimized Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC)

The **Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC)** measures how much solar heat passes through a window.

The ideal SHGC value depends on climate:

- **Lower SHGC values** help keep homes cooler in warm climates  
- **Higher SHGC values** may benefit colder climates where solar heat gain can warm interiors  

Simonton offers **region-specific glass packages** designed to optimize SHGC performance based on climate zone requirements.

---

## 7. Insulated Vinyl Window Frames

Frame material also plays a major role in window efficiency.

Vinyl frames are popular for energy-efficient windows because they provide natural insulation and resist heat transfer better than many metal frame systems.

Simonton windows use **insulated vinyl frames** that help reduce thermal bridging and maintain consistent indoor temperatures.

Vinyl frames also resist moisture, corrosion, and expansion due to weather exposure.

---

## 8. Spacer Systems That Reduce Heat Transfer

Spacer systems separate the panes of glass in insulated window units. In older windows, metal spacers could conduct heat, reducing overall efficiency.

Modern windows often use **warm-edge spacer systems** designed to reduce heat transfer at the edge of the glass.

Simonton windows incorporate spacer technologies designed to:

- Improve thermal performance  
- Reduce condensation  
- Enhance long-term durability of insulated glass units  

---

## 9. Climate-Zone Specific Glass Packages

The United States has multiple climate zones, and the ideal window configuration varies depending on the region.

ENERGY STAR requirements are based on these climate zones, meaning window performance standards differ between northern and southern regions.

Simonton offers **glass packages tailored to regional ENERGY STAR climate zones**, helping homeowners choose windows that perform effectively in their local environment.

---

## 10. Potential Energy Savings and Tax Incentives

Energy-efficient windows can reduce heating and cooling costs by improving the insulation of a home.

ENERGY STAR estimates that upgrading to ENERGY STAR certified windows may reduce household energy bills by around **12% nationwide**, depending on the home and climate.

In some cases, homeowners may also qualify for **federal energy efficiency tax credits** when installing qualifying windows.

Certain Simonton window products are designed to meet these performance requirements.

# Common Questions About Energy Efficient Windows

## Are Simonton windows energy efficient?
